### 1. Advertising Compliance (North America – General Principles)

| # | Requirement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Avoid absolute superlatives** (e.g., “best,” “world’s finest,” “#1”) unless backed by verifiable, documented evidence (e.g., certified lab tests, independent surveys). Even “better” can trigger scrutiny if comparative. | Common sense – not legal advice. The FTC and Ad Standards in Canada enforce substantiation. |
| 2 | **Price displays must be clear and inclusive** | Any “sale” price must be compared to a genuine reference price (e.g., “Was $XX, now $YY”). Do not use strikethrough prices that were never actually charged. Shipping, taxes, and fees must be disclosed before checkout. |
| 3 | **Promotional disclosures (e.g., “limited time,” “free shipping”)** must be prominent, unambiguous, and not hidden in fine print. | Example: “Free shipping on orders over $50” – show the minimum spend clearly. Avoid “free” if any non‑optional fee exists. |
| 4 | **Testimonials and endorsements** must reflect honest opinions and disclose any material connection (e.g., free product, payment). | Even influencer posts need clear #ad or #sponsored. |
| 5 | **Environmental claims (e.g., “eco-friendly,” “biodegradable”)** must be specific and substantiated. Avoid vague terms like “green” without context. | The FTC’s Green Guides apply. For a pour‑over set, if you claim “reusable filter reduces waste,” be ready to explain how. |

### 2. Cultural Taboos & Sensitivity Checks (North America)

- **Colors**: No major taboos. Red is generally positive (energy, passion). Black is associated with elegance but also mourning – fine for product packaging. Avoid overuse of yellow in a way that might be seen as “cheap” (not a taboo, just a perception).  
- **Numbers**: 13 is considered unlucky by many; avoid 13‑piece sets or pricing at $13.13. 7 is lucky (e.g., “7‑step brewing”).  
- **Gestures**: The “OK” hand sign (👌) is widely used as positive, but be aware some fringe groups have co‑opted it; in mainstream advertising still safe. Thumbs up is universally positive. Avoid pointing fingers directly at the viewer aggressively.  
- **Religion**: Do not associate coffee with religious rituals (e.g., “like a sacred communion”). Avoid imagery of crosses, churches, or “blessed” unless appropriate. Coffee is secular.  
- **Humor**: Dry, self‑deprecating humor works well. Avoid sarcasm that could be misinterpreted as rudeness. Do not mock any ethnicity, gender, or body type.  
- **Gender & Roles**: Avoid stereotyping – e.g., showing only women making coffee for the family. Show diversity (different genders, ages, ethnicities) enjoying the product.  
- **Cultural appropriation**: If using “traditional” Japanese pour‑over techniques (e.g., Hario V60), give credit and avoid claiming “ancient secret.” Be respectful of origins.  
- **Health claims**: Do not imply coffee cures diseases or dramatically improves health without FDA‑approved wording. “Antioxidants” is fine if supported, but don’t say “reduces cancer risk.”

### 4. Pre‑Publishing Checklist

- [ ] Have I removed any absolute claims (e.g., “best,” “perfect”) without proof?  
- [ ] Are all prices, discounts, and “was” prices clearly displayed and not misleading?  
- [ ] Is any “free” offer truly free (no hidden fees)?  
- [ ] Are all testimonials real, and have I disclosed any material connections?  
- [ ] Have I avoided any religious, political, or cultural stereotypes?  
- [ ] Are all environmental claims (e.g., “reusable,” “eco‑friendly”) specific and substantiated?  
- [ ] Have I checked that the imagery includes diverse representation (gender, ethnicity, age)?  
- [ ] Is the humor appropriate for a broad audience (no sarcasm that could offend)?  
- [ ] Have I included a clear privacy policy link if collecting any user data?  
- [ ] Have I double‑checked all spelling, grammar, and locale‑specific terms (e.g., “color” vs “colour” – use US English)?
